You remember, don't you

The particular gold of late afternoon light through a car window on a drive home from somewhere ordinary. The specific quiet of a house on a Sunday morning before anyone else was awake. A voice calling your name from another room, the exact pitch of it, gone now, but not really — still filed somewhere in you, ready to surface.

There's a version of you that existed in that light, in that quiet, that hasn't gone anywhere either. It's still there, underneath who you've become, the way a house keeps its old foundation even after every room has been remodeled.

You didn't know, back then, that you'd miss it this specifically — not the big events, but the unremarkable ones. The walk to a bus stop. A song playing from someone else's radio. The smell of a particular season turning.

That's the strange gift of time: it doesn't just take things, it also seals them, perfectly, exactly as they were, so you can return to them later and find them untouched.

So go back for a moment. Let yourself stand in that old light again. It's still warm. It was always going to still be warm.
